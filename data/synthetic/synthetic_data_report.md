## Assumptions

- **Catalogue only.** Products, prices and product IDs come from `data/processed/selected_products.csv`; `unit_price` is the catalogue `discount_price` (no price changes over time).
- **Quantity ≠ frequency.** Each `order_items` row is one purchase occasion of one product. `quantity` is the number of catalogue packs bought (e.g. 3 × 500 ml milk) and never creates extra occasions.
- **Consumption-rate model.** Each product type has a base interval and base quantity for a 3-person household. A customer's personal interval = base × segment multiplier × personal noise (lognormal). Quantity scales with household size and with interval/base, so bulk and weekly buyers buy more per trip.
- **Imperfect periodicity.** Every gap is multiplied by lognormal noise (σ = product CV + segment CV); occasions can be skipped; ~25% of customers take a 6–16 day break (purchases resume afterwards).
- **Orders = shopping trips.** A customer's product purchases within `order_window_days` are merged into one order dated on the first day; weekly buyers' trips snap to their preferred weekday. At most one order per customer per day; a product appears at most once per order (quantities combined).
- **Subsets.** Each customer buys a random subset of products (size by segment, weighted by product `adoption`).
- **Bread** is the catalogue's rusk substitute (see Phase 1) but is simulated with a bread-like frequent cycle, as specified for this phase.
- **Segments** (`behaviour_segment` in customers.csv) and `customer_product_profiles.csv` are generator ground truth for evaluating a future algorithm. They should not be used as model inputs.
- **Not modelled:** seasonality, promotions, price changes, stock-outs, cancellations/returns, substitution between brands.

## Segment definitions

| Segment | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| regular | Frequent, fairly regular buyers; many products; near-natural intervals. |
| weekly | One shopping trip per week on a preferred weekday; frequent items bought in weekly quantities. |
| bulk | Intervals ×2.5 with proportionally larger quantities per occasion; purchases within 4 days merged into one trip. |
| irregular | Longer, highly variable gaps (interval ×1.6, high noise) and 30% skipped occasions. |
| new | Signed up 7–45 days before the end date; 1–few purchases per product. |
| lapsed | Regular history that stops entirely 30–80 days before the end date. |
| basket_consolidator | Merges purchases within 5 days into one large multi-product order. |
